## Output-profile diagnosis

[`paint-calibration-26.930.31730.json`](paint-calibration-26.930.31730.json)
and its two tiny PNGs are project-authored CSS rendered in temporary
`about:blank` targets, not copied product UI. Under the default display ICC
profile, half-opacity white over `#242424` yields raw 144 and ColorSync sRGB
155. An opaque `rgb(145,145,145)` control yields raw 134 and normalized 145;
the background yields raw 33 and normalized 36. With
`--force-color-profile=srgb`, the same three points are 145/145/36, untagged.

This isolates composition in the output profile: post-hoc ICC conversion can
correct opaque colors but cannot undo alpha compositing. Do not compensate by
changing observed product opacity or color. Historical native-profile crops
remain intact; new product/replay comparisons must independently pass the
same three-point sRGB calibration. The probe's launch flag is capture provenance,
not a product stylesheet modification or an observation of the user's default
display rendering.

Portable research checks authenticate both PNGs, their ICC identity and raw
pixels, plus the untagged sRGB controls. They explicitly do **not** claim a
native transform. `pnpm check:paint-calibration:native` reproduces the ColorSync
transform on macOS and fails closed elsewhere; every macOS acceptance CI shard
requires that command. This separation fixes the Ubuntu quality job without
dropping native calibration or introducing a substitute color transform.
